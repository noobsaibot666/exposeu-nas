# Release Content Kit Positioning

## Positioning summary

**Release Content Kit** is a done-for-you photo and video content session for artists preparing a single, EP, album, show, tour announcement, music video launch, or new artist profile.

The offer is not positioned as an EPK, press kit, or marketing package. It is positioned as the visual content artists need before they release: press photos, portraits, short clips, social assets, release visuals, behind-the-scenes content, and high-res files that can be used across social, streaming, booking, press, posters, and campaign channels.

Core angle:

> Photo and video content for your next release.

The page should make the artist feel the real problem immediately:

> I have a release coming. expose.u can create the photos and videos I need to promote it.

## Best expose.u positioning

Use this hierarchy:

### Main offer

**Release Content Kit**

### Supporting line

Photo and video content for musicians and artists preparing a single, album, show, tour, or campaign release.

### Practical value

Photos, short clips, social assets, press photos, platform visuals, release campaign assets, and print-ready files built around the artist's release, sound, identity, and timeline.

### Sales angle

The artist brings the release. expose.u builds the visual assets around it.

## Audience

The page speaks to artists who already know something important is coming, but do not yet have enough content to support it.

## Bilingual message alignment

English and German should preserve these message points:

- The artist has a release coming and needs clear photo and video content for promotion.
- The service should be understandable in 5 seconds without relying on poetic framing.
- The offer gives photos, short clips, and social/platform assets built around the campaign before release day.
- expose.u speaks as artists to artists, not as a corporate agency.
- The kit adapts to the artist's release, sound, identity, aesthetic, and timeline.
- Assets are useful across Instagram, TikTok, Spotify, YouTube, press, booking, posters, print, and digital campaigns.
- The EPK and press kit remain use cases for the assets, not the core product promise.

Acceptable localization differences:

- "We've been where you are" and "Wir kennen das Gefühl" are not literal matches, but both communicate artist empathy.
- "Your image is always on" and "Dein Auftritt läuft immer" are not literal matches, but both communicate that the artist's public presence is always being judged.
- "Tell us about your release" and "Erzähl uns von deinem Release" both frame the CTA around the artist's concrete release moment.

## Role of the EPK

The EPK is a use case, not the product.

Correct framing:

> Get the visuals you need for your release campaign, press, social media, booking, and EPK.

Avoid framing:

> We create your EPK.

The page can mention press kits, booking, and media assets, but it should not promise full PR, music marketing strategy, playlist pitching, campaign management, or a complete electronic press kit.

## What to avoid

Avoid making the page too much about:

- EPKs
- Media kits
- Press kits
- PDFs
- Corporate package language
- Long explanations
- Over-promising music marketing outcomes
- Strategy work that exposes expose.u as a PR agency rather than a visual production team

## Positioning check

The current page positioning is correct because it:

- Leads with the artist's real release problem: content is not ready.
- Names the practical assets artists can immediately use.
- Keeps the offer rooted in expose.u's strength: visual production.
- Uses artist-to-artist language instead of agency language.
- Treats EPKs, booking, press, and platforms as destinations for the assets, not as the product itself.
- Ends with a simple conversion action: start the release kit.
