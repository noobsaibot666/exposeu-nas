## Purpose

Review expose.u standalone landing pages against the current marketing positioning:

> Editorial visual documentation for culture, spaces and experiences.

This document evaluates each landing page for:

- Strategic fit
- Buyer clarity
- Psychological logic
- Copy quality
- Sequence / structure
- Amount of information
- Image direction
- Client conversion potential
- Immediate changes

## Method Note

Some live pages could not be fully parsed by automated web tools. This audit is therefore based on:

- The supplied landing page structure and URLs

The audit should be treated as a strategic and conversion review, not a line-by-line SEO crawl.

# GLOBAL LANDING PAGE RULES

## What every landing page must do

A landing page has one job:

> Convert one specific visitor with one specific problem into one action.

Do not use landing pages like generic service pages.

Each page should answer, fast:

1. Who is this for?
2. What problem do they have?
3. What outcome do we create?
4. What assets do they receive?
5. Why expose.u?
6. What should they do next?

## Ideal landing page sequence

1. Hero: direct segment-specific promise
2. Pain: what the client risks losing
3. Outcome: what documentation enables
4. Assets: what they receive and where it is used
5. Proof / visual credibility
6. Process: simple 3 steps
7. CTA
8. Secondary trust signals

## Global copy rule

Use fewer abstract words.

More:
- Press
- Archive
- Case study
- Social assets
- Award submissions
- Booking assets
- Future promotion
- Collector communication
- Sponsor reports
- Website updates

Less:
- Moments
- Stories
- Creativity
- Memories
- Content creation
- Capture
- Beautiful visuals

## Global image rule

Every landing page needs images that prove the promise.

Do not use beautiful but ambiguous images.

Images should immediately signal the segment:
- Gallery page: installation, opening, artwork, visitors
- Agency page: spatial experience, activation, installation, interaction
- Artist page: portrait, release, studio, press identity
- Pop-up page: temporary space, product/food/fashion, audience interaction
- Concert page: stage, crowd, BTS, vertical motion
- Release page: artist identity, cover mood, campaign assets

# PAGE 01 — GALLERY / MUSEUM DOCUMENTATION

URLs:
- /gallery-museum-documentation
- /de/gallery-museum-documentation

## Strategic Fit

Rating: 9.5/10

This is one of the strongest landing pages expose.u can build.

It directly serves the strongest cultural segment:
- galleries
- museums
- curators
- cultural institutions
- foundations
- project spaces

This page should be a flagship paid campaign page.

## Core Client Problem

The exhibition is temporary, but the need for communication continues.

After the exhibition opens, galleries and institutions still need:
- press images
- archive material
- website updates
- collector communication
- grant / funding reports
- social documentation
- future exhibition proof

## Best Hero Direction

Use a clear outcome-led headline.

Recommended hero:

> Exhibition documentation for galleries, museums and cultural spaces.

Subheadline:

> Press-ready photo and video assets for exhibitions, openings, archives and long-term communication.

CTA:

> Check availability

## Psychological Logic

The strongest driver is not "beautiful documentation."

The strongest driver is loss aversion:

> If the exhibition is not documented well, the work disappears from future communication.

The page should make this clear without sounding dramatic.

## What to Add

- A section titled: "What documentation supports"
- Press
- Archive
- Collector communication
- Social media
- Grant reporting
- Website updates

## What to Remove / Avoid

- Avoid making this sound like event photography.
- Avoid over-talking about "artistic sensitivity" without operational value.
- Avoid long paragraphs about philosophy.
- Avoid generic "we capture your exhibition."

## Recommended Page Structure

### 1. Hero

Exhibition documentation for galleries, museums and cultural spaces.

### 2. Why it matters

An exhibition exists for a limited time. Its documentation becomes the record used for press, archives, collectors and future communication.

### 3. Assets delivered

- Installation views
- Artwork details
- Opening documentation
- Visitor atmosphere
- Press-ready selects
- Web and social exports
- Archive-ready files

### 4. Built for

- Gallery websites
- Press releases
- Collector updates
- Grant and funding reports
- Exhibition archives
- Social media communication

### 5. Why expose.u

- Quiet presence
- Editorial eye
- Understanding of space
- Respect for artwork
- Photo + video
- Berlin based

### 6. CTA

Check availability for your exhibition.

## Amount of Information

Ideal length: medium.

This buyer needs clarity and trust, not a sales essay.

Keep the page shorter than a canonical service page.

## Image Direction

Use:
- Clean installation views
- People moving through space
- Curator / artist moments
- Detail shots
- Opening atmosphere

Avoid:
- Overly cinematic images that hide the artwork
- Generic event crowd shots
- Images that look like parties

## Immediate Changes

- Make the hero more direct.
- Add "press-ready" near the top.
- Add "archive" near the top.
- Add practical use cases before philosophy.
- Add a short "What you receive" section.
- Keep public pricing off the page.

## Conversion Potential

High.

This page is highly aligned with the market and should be used for Meta ads, direct gallery outreach and SEO testing.

# BRUTAL CONCLUSION

## Strongest pages to advertise now

1. Gallery/Museum Documentation
2. Brand & Agency Documentation

- Concerts Berlin
- Pop-ups

They can easily become generic event pages.

## Biggest global weakness

The danger is not weak services.

The danger is unclear segmentation.

Each landing page must have one buyer, one pain, one outcome, one CTA.

## Immediate Global Action List

- Remove generic event/content language.
- Put concrete use cases above the fold.
- Add "what the assets are used for" on every page.

- Make Brand & Agency sharper and less cultural.
- Make Gallery/Museum more institutional.
- Make Pop-ups more brand/design/culture, less events.
- Make Concerts about momentum and future promotion.
