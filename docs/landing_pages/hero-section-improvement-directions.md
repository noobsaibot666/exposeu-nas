# Hero Section Improvement Directions for Dev AI

## Goal

Improve the hero section of the landing page so a first-time visitor immediately understands:

1. What the service is
2. Who it is for
3. What result it delivers
4. Why it feels credible
5. What action they should take next

The hero should sell the service clearly within the first 5 seconds.

---

## Core Principle

The hero must use the language of the customer, not internal business language.

Avoid vague or broad wording such as:

- Creative content services
- Media solutions
- Visual storytelling
- Artist media kit
- Professional content production

Use specific wording that tells the exact audience what they get and why it matters.

Example direction:

> Photo and video content for artists preparing a single, album, show, tour, or campaign release.

This is clearer because it names the audience, the service, and the situation.

---

## Hero Section Requirements

### 1. Headline

The headline must say what the service does in clear customer language.

It should be specific, direct, and easy to understand.

Recommended headline:

> Release Content Kit for Artists

Alternative headline:

> Photo and Video Content for Your Next Release

Avoid abstract headlines that sound poetic but do not explain the offer.

---

### 2. Subheadline

The subheadline must define the target customer and the concrete outcome.

Recommended subheadline:

> A focused photo and video session for musicians, performers, and creators preparing a single, album, show, tour, or campaign.

Alternative subheadline:

> Get press photos, short clips, and social media assets ready to use across release campaigns, booking, press, and promotion.

Keep it short. Do not use a long paragraph in the hero.

---

### 3. Visual Proof

The hero should show real visual proof of the service, not generic illustrations or decorative imagery.

Use one of the following:

- A real artist portrait from expose.u
- A real behind-the-scenes shooting image
- A grid of actual deliverables: press photo, vertical video frame, social crop, campaign visual
- A mockup showing how the assets appear across Instagram, press, Spotify/profile, and poster formats

Best option:

> Show a small deliverables grid above the fold with real examples of what the artist receives.

The visual should make the offer feel tangible.

Avoid generic images of people smiling, abstract graphics, or visuals that do not show the actual service output.

---

### 4. CTA

The main CTA should be specific and action-oriented.

Recommended CTA:

> Tell us about your release

Alternative CTAs:

- Book a Release Session
- Plan Your Release Content
- Get Your Content Kit

Avoid generic CTAs such as:

- Learn More
- Get Started
- Contact Us

Add a secondary CTA if useful:

> See what’s included

---

### 5. Immediate Social Proof

Add a proof element above the fold, close to the CTA.

Use one of the following:

- Logos of artists, venues, or collaborators
- A short credibility line
- A concrete number if available
- A short testimonial snippet
- A location/status proof line

Examples:

> Berlin-based photo and video studio for artists, venues, and cultural projects.

> Trusted by artists, performers, and cultural spaces in Berlin.

> Created for musicians, performers, DJs, and independent artists preparing their next release.

If logos are available, place them below the CTA as a compact logo row.

---

## Recommended Hero Structure

Use this structure:

```txt
[Small eyebrow]
Release Content Kit

[Headline]
Photo and video content for your next release.

[Subheadline]
For musicians, performers, and creators preparing a single, album, show, tour, or campaign. Get press photos, short clips, and social assets ready for promotion.

[CTA group]
Primary: Tell us about your release
Secondary: See what’s included

[Proof line]
Berlin-based studio creating visual content for artists, performances, and cultural projects.

[Visual proof]
Real image grid or deliverables preview showing press photos, vertical clips, social crops, and campaign visuals.
```

---

## Copy Rules

Keep the hero short and specific.

### Do

- Say exactly what the service is
- Name the audience clearly
- Mention the use case: release, show, tour, campaign
- Mention concrete deliverables: press photos, short clips, social assets
- Use simple international English
- Keep sentences short
- Make the CTA specific

### Do not

- Explain the full EPK concept in the hero
- Use long paragraphs
- Use internal jargon
- Overuse words like storytelling, media, strategy, or creative solutions
- Make it sound like a brochure
- Use generic stock-style visuals
- Promise marketing results that expose.u cannot guarantee

---

## Main Message to Communicate

The artist should immediately understand this:

> I have a release coming. expose.u can create the photos and videos I need to promote it.

That is the core value.

---

## 5-Second Test

After updating the hero, test it with this question:

> If everything else on the landing page was hidden, would a stranger from the target audience understand what is being sold in 5 seconds?

If the answer is no, simplify the hero further.

The hero should not make people decode the offer. It should make the right artist feel:

> This was made for me.
